# WHAT IS ACTUALLY AT STAKE

## The settlement that exists does NOT cover streaming
- Late June 2026: the **major labels, NMPA, A2IM, NSAI and the Music Artists Coalition** proposed
  a settlement stating existing Phono IV rates *"should not be amended except for continuing
  inflation adjustments to the rates for **physical phonorecords and permanent downloads**."*
- **That settlement covers physical formats, permanent downloads and ringtones ONLY.**
  **The INTERACTIVE STREAMING mechanical rate for 2028-2032 is explicitly UNSETTLED** — which is
  precisely why the schedule has non-settling parties filing Written Direct Statements Oct 5.

## The bundling loophole — the money
- March 2024: Spotify added audiobooks to Premium and reclassified it as a **BUNDLE**, lowering
  its US mechanical obligation. NMPA: this *"forced the conversion of over 44 million subscribers
  into bundle platforms that those subscribers did not request."*
- **NMPA quantifies the damage at ~$230M to publishers in year one** (initial estimate was ~$150M)
  and **~$3.1 BILLION through 2032** — i.e. across exactly the Phono V period.
- Digital Music News on the Phono IV settlement: it *"on the streaming side **enabled a bundling
  craze that caused mechanicals to plummet**"* — described as *"a disaster."*
- Litigation: **The MLC v. Spotify (SDNY 1:24-cv-03809)**; Spotify discloses **~€473M** exposure.

## Precedent that the CRB will not rubber-stamp
- **In 2022 the CRB REJECTED a proposed mechanical-rate freeze**, describing *"the vertical
  integration linking music publishers and record labels"* as **"a warning flag."**
- The same objection is live again. **Eight Mile Style** (Eminem's publisher) calls the current
  proposal *"effectively a rate freeze, unmoored from economic reality and for which no valid
  justification exists,"* citing an *"oligopsony"* and a *"walled garden where the 'willing
  buyers' and 'willing sellers' are the same at the corporate level."*
- **Songwriters Guild of America + Word Collections** filed a joint objection calling the proposal
  *"demonstrably unreasonable"* and asking the CRB to **deny** it. Their catch: the proposal would
  **reset the base rate DOWN to 12 cents on Jan 1 2028** rather than continuing from 13.1 cents.
- Comment period closed **August 10, 2026**.

# WHY THIS FITS THE BRIEF
This is **not** a valuation story. It is a **statutorily-scheduled regulatory proceeding** that
will reset the single largest variable cost line of a company trading at ~34.5x forward with
**ZERO sell ratings** and a gross margin that has **plateaued at ~33% for four straight quarters**
— because, per Spotify's own filings, royalties are *"the greater of a percentage of relevant
revenue and a per user amount,"* so the cost line **cannot operating-leverage.**

**The market is modelling Spotify's gross margin expanding toward management's 35-40% 2030 target.
The CRB is simultaneously running a proceeding that could move the largest input cost the other
way, on a published calendar, with an initial determination due by December 17, 2027.**

## What still has to be proven (handed to the deep-dive agent)
1. **Sizing.** US mechanical royalties are the PUBLISHING side only (~10-15% of revenue). The
   LABEL/master side (~50-55%) is separately negotiated and is NOT set by the CRB. **Do not
   conflate them.** How much of Spotify's total COGS does the CRB actually control?
2. What rate are publishers likely to demand on Oct 5, and what is a realistic 2028 outcome?
3. Does Spotify pass it through via price increases? (It has raised price repeatedly — but the
   Sept 2025 and Feb 2026 hikes lap in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, so the pass-through lever is
   already partly spent.)
4. Has ANY sell-side analyst modelled Phono V? Does Spotify's 20-F disclose it as a risk factor?
   **If nobody is modelling a 2028 reset, that is the edge.**
