## ⭐ THE FINDING: trailing earnings are inflated by a ~$4.4B ONE-OFF
- **"Other income (expense) – net" = $4,842M in 6M-2026 vs $234M in 6M-2025** — a **$4.6B swing.**
- Cash flow statement: **"(Gains) losses on purchases and sales of business interests" = $(4,428)M.**
- => **Roughly $4.4B of the $5,413M six-month net income is a one-time gain on business
  dispositions.** Q1 2026 carried ~$4,745M of net income (~$17.49 EPS); Q2 alone was $668M / $2.47.

## ⚠️ THEREFORE: DO NOT USE THE "FORWARD P/E > TRAILING P/E MEANS CONSENSUS EXPECTS DECLINE" ARGUMENT
That reading (fwd 44.5 vs trailing 27.3) is a **DATA ARTIFACT** created by the disposition gain
sitting in the trailing base. It is not evidence that the street models an earnings decline.
Any pitch using it would be dismantled in Q&A. **Killed this argument before it reached the memo.**

## What it does legitimately support
Strip the one-off and GEV's clean earnings power is far below the headline:
- Q2 2026 clean diluted EPS **$2.47** → naive annualization ≈ **$9.9**
- At $953.09 that is **~96x clean run-rate earnings**
- Consensus forward P/E of ~44.5 implies forward EPS of roughly **$21.4** — i.e. the street
  underwrites roughly **2x the current quarterly run-rate.**
⚠️ Caveat before using this: GEV has real seasonality (H2-weighted deliveries) and genuine
growth, so a naive Q2 annualization understates it. **The real work is to bridge Q2 clean EPS
to the implied ~$21.4 forward number and test whether that bridge is achievable.** That bridge
IS the pitch if the gap doesn't close credibly.
