### The landmark stat
- US adult obesity rate: **39.9% (2022 peak) → 36.4% (2026)** = **-3.5pp**
- First sustained multi-year decline in modern tracking history.
- **US diabetes rate has been FLAT since 2023, after 15 years of continuous increase.**
  (Underrated corroborating datapoint — harder to fake than self-reported weight.)

## ⚠️ THE SKEPTIC'S CASE — judges WILL attack this, so own it first
Source: https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/kennedy-distorts-stat-in-celebrating-obesity-drop/
1. Gallup is **self-reported** height/weight → systematically understates obesity.
2. CDC **NHANES is measured, not self-reported, and is the authoritative series.**
   Latest NHANES (2021–2023): 40.3% vs 41.9% (2017–2020) — **within margin of error,
   NOT statistically significant.**
3. **About two-thirds of the Gallup decline occurred 2022–2024**, i.e. BEFORE the
   biggest GLP-1 scale-up. So the causal attribution to GLP-1s is not clean.
4. Prior apparent obesity declines have reversed.
=> Defensible framing: *directionally* obesity has likely turned, but the magnitude
   and the GLP-1 attribution are genuinely uncertain. **Do not build a pitch that
   requires the 3.5pp number to be literally true.** Build one that works on
   prescription volumes, which are hard data.

## Behavior change among users — PwC 2026 GLP-1 Usage & Attitudes Survey
Source: https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/consumer-markets/library/glp-1-consumer-trends.html
- **61%** of current users buying fewer sweet treats
- **56%** buying fewer salty snacks
- Non-alcoholic sugary drinks contracting; functional beverages / water growing
- 41% of consumers (all, not just users) say they are avoiding impulse purchases
- C-store salty snacks: decline driven by **large-format bags** losing to
  portion-controlled formats — a MIX problem, not pure category exit

## Apparel: the effect is BIDIRECTIONAL (this is the key nuance)
### Tailwind — "re-wardrobing"
- Bernstein: GLP-1 wardrobe replacement = **+$3B to $13B/yr** of incremental US apparel spend
- Circana (Mar 2026): 80% of users anticipate wardrobe change; 55% already purchased
- Dentsu: 50% of US GLP-1 users shopping for clothes more often; 29% buying more accessories
- Stitch Fix (SFIX) Q2 call, Mar 11 2026: customer mentions of weight loss **tripled over
  two years, +75% YoY in the quarter**; launched a dedicated GLP-1 stylist program
### Headwind — plus-size TAM is structurally shrinking
- **Destination XL (DXLG)**: mgmt says **~25% of its customers are on GLP-1s**, citing
  "structural change in demand within the big-and-tall category."
  Q1 FY26 (ended May 2 2026): sales $103.3M (-2.1%), comps -3.8%, net loss $5.9M,
  adj. EBITDA -$0.7M. Missed consensus $107.9M.
- **Torrid (CURV)**: 171 store closures since program inception; Q1 FY26 net income
  $0.4M vs $5.9M LY; gross margin 35.3%; **debt $301.2M vs cash $22.8M**.
- Mainstream retailers CUTTING plus-size assortment: Nike, Old Navy, L.L. Bean,
  Ralph Lauren, Shein, H&M (H&M plus-size fit coverage 86% Feb-2025 → 50% Dec-2025).

## Why the "Victoria's Secret was killed by GLP-1s" premise is FALSE
- VSCO/VSXY Q1 FY26 (June 2, 2026): beat + raised guidance, **stock +40-47% in a day.**
- Barclays' Adrienne Yih asked directly about GLP-1 sizing impact.
  CEO Hillary Super: **"only minimal impact"** on sizing trends.
- Bras (the most sizing-sensitive category) posted **double-digit comp growth.**
=> The seed premise is a media narrative, not a fact. Useful as a *framing device*
   ("the market gets the sign wrong on GLP-1 second-order effects") but not as a thesis.
