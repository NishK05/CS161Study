## ⭐⭐ THIS KILLS THE GLP-1 ANGLE ON NCLH — IN BOTH DIRECTIONS
**1. The "GLP-1 cuts food cost = margin tailwind" thesis is refuted.**
- **Food expense GREW +7.4% YoY**, faster than the +4.9% revenue growth. No deflation whatsoever.
- Food is **$87,322 / $2,640,544 = 3.3% of revenue** and **5.5% of cruise operating expense.**
- Even a heroic 20% decline in food consumption = ~$17M/quarter = **~0.7% of revenue.**
  Immaterial, and swamped by fuel, which moved **+39.4%** in the same quarter.

**2. The "GLP-1 cuts onboard alcohol/casino spend" thesis is refuted.**
- **Onboard and other revenue GREW +12.6%** — the highest-margin line is ACCELERATING, and
  growing 10x faster than passenger ticket revenue (+1.2%).
- Corroborates the separate Gallup finding that US drinking has NOT declined (54% drink, flat;
  drinks/week UP 2.8 -> 3.2).

=> **If NCLH is the pick, it must be pitched on leverage, yields, and cost inflation.
   Do NOT put GLP-1 in an NCLH memo. Primary data contradicts it both ways.**

## ⭐ WHAT THE FILING ACTUALLY SHOWS: LOW EARNINGS QUALITY
- **Operating income FELL 14.3%** in Q2 despite +4.9% revenue, because costs ran far ahead:
  fuel +39.4%, payroll +14.0%, D&A +11.3%, total cruise opex +8.9%.
- **Net income "grew" from $30.0M to $222.6M almost entirely BELOW the operating line** —
  a $65.9M reduction in net interest expense plus a $189.9M swing in "other income (expense),
  net" (from -$156.4M to +$33.5M).
- Diluted share count is **UP 3.5% YoY** — dilution, not buyback.
=> **The headline EPS improvement is not operational.** That is a genuine, checkable analytical
   point and exactly the kind of thing the rules mean by "technical rather than purely subjective."

### ⚠️ CORRECTION TO MY OWN READ ABOVE
I compared advance ticket sales to **December 31, 2025** ($3,651M vs $3,201M = +14.1%) and called
forward bookings "healthy." **That comparison is SEASONAL, not year-over-year, and it is wrong.**
The correct YoY comparison at the June balance date is:
| | Jun 2025 | Jun 2026 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NCLH deferred revenue** | **$3,834M** | **$3,651M** | **−4.8%** |
...and that is on **+8.9% capacity growth**, i.e. roughly **−12% per berth-day.**
Meanwhile **RCL +5.6%** and **CCL +4.6%** on the same measure.
**=> This is company-specific share loss, not an industry or consumer problem.**
It inverts my "strongest item in the bull case" line. Forward bookings are the BEAR case.
