## ⭐⭐ THE FINDING THAT FALSIFIES MY EARLIER CLAIM — and it is the best GLP-1 insight in the project
I told the team that GLP-1 users exercise more (William Blair survey: gym membership among users
**24% → 35%**; under-45 **43% → 62%**), and framed that as a demand tailwind for athletic footwear.
**Objective wearable data says the opposite.**

**ENDO 2026 (Endocrine Society annual meeting, Chicago; presented June 13, 2026)
— NIH *All of Us* + Fitbit, the first large wearable-based study of its kind:**
- n=1,950 adults with obesity starting a GLP-1; **753 with sufficient wearable data**; 78.6% women; mean age 52.7
- **Daily steps: 5,047 → 4,487 = −11.1%**
- **Moderate-to-vigorous physical activity: 28 → 22 min/day = −21.4%**
- Largest declines in men and in those with joint/muscle pain
- *"Importantly, the researchers found **no evidence that losing weight with these medications led
  people to become more physically active**."*
- Lead author Sajana Maharjan, M.D.: *"**While many assume that weight loss leads naturally to
  increased physical activity, our study suggests otherwise.**"*
- Source: sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260614011841.htm

**Reconciling the two:** the survey asks *"did you join a gym?"*; the Fitbit measures *"did you
move?"* **Joining a gym is not using it.** Self-reported intent ≠ measured behaviour.
**=> Hard data beats survey data. Athletic footwear replacement is MILEAGE-DRIVEN, so a cohort
reducing MVPA 21% and steps 11% is a direct, measurable reduction in replacement demand.**
This is a genuinely differentiated, citable, falsifiable insight — and it points NEGATIVE for Nike.

NOT the tariffs paid — it **understates** the run-rate. **Consensus FY27 of $1.71 therefore implies
a −19% decline off a clean base.** Consensus is already bearish; the risk is it is not bearish enough.
