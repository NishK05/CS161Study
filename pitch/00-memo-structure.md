# Citadel 2-Page Memo — Structure & Judging Logic

## The constraint
**2 pages MAX, including appendix.** Plus a separate model. 3-12 month horizon.
Every team pitches from the same 8 names => **idea sourcing is worth zero.**
100% of the differentiation is: analytical depth + differentiated data + a QUANTIFIED
variant vs. a consensus you have actually measured.

## What must be on page 1
1. **Recommendation line** (one sentence): LONG/SHORT [TICKER] at $X, target $Y (+/-Z%),
   3-12 month horizon. Include the risk/reward ratio explicitly.
2. **Variant perception** (the whole pitch, in 3 sentences):
   - What consensus believes — stated as a NUMBER (consensus EPS/revenue/margin, not a vibe)
   - What we believe — also a number
   - **The gap, and WHY it exists** (why is the market wrong? what do we see that they don't?)
3. **The 2-3 supporting pillars**, each with hard evidence. One chart maximum.
4. **Catalyst path with DATES.** This is non-negotiable at a 3-12 month horizon.
   Earnings dates, guidance events, data releases, contract decisions.
5. **Valuation bridge:** current multiple -> target multiple, and current EPS -> our EPS.
   Show the arithmetic. No DCF terminal value — it is a horizon mismatch and gets marked down.

## What must be on page 2 (appendix)
- Bull / base / bear with explicit EPS and multiple assumptions and probabilities
- **Risks and what would falsify us** (judges reward intellectual honesty here; a memo with
  no real risk section reads as naive)
- Positioning: short interest, days to cover, borrow, consensus rating distribution & PT
- Key model outputs / the one table that carries the thesis

## Scoring heuristics to optimize for
- **Falsifiability.** State what would prove us wrong and by when. This is the single most
  underrated signal of buy-side maturity.
- **Quantify the variant.** "We are 12% below consensus FY27 EBIT" beats "we think margins
  are at risk."
- **Own the bear case first.** If we have a data weakness (e.g. self-reported Gallup obesity
  data), raise and neutralize it before a judge does.
- **Know the positioning.** Pitching a short in a name with 18% short interest and 5.7 days
  to cover (NCLH) without addressing squeeze risk is disqualifying.
- **Second-order thinking.** The strongest available meta-insight from our research:
  *"the demand thesis was right; the capture thesis was wrong"* — correctly identifying a
  demand shift tells you almost nothing about the stock unless you also have a
  moat/pricing/margin argument.

## Selection criteria for WHICH name to pitch
Rank on: (variant perception strength) x (catalyst density in window) x (positioning asymmetry)
x (our ability to source differentiated data).
Deduct heavily for: crowded positioning, consensus PT already at/below spot with no catalyst,
and any thesis that needs a >12-month horizon to pay off.
